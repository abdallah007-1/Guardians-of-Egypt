One campaign. Three promises to the past.

Hidden places · Untold stories
Campaign 01

EGYPT UNCOVERED

Hidden places. Untold stories.

Egypt is far bigger than its postcard. This campaign pushes beyond the famous icons towards places where you can stand almost alone with 4,000 years of history.

Where to go instead

  • Beni Hassan & Tell el-Amarna (Minya) — rock-cut tombs and a whole abandoned capital.
  • Tanis & Bubastis (Delta) — royal tombs and cat temples far from the crowds.
  • Wadi Al-Hitan (Fayoum) — a UNESCO natural site of fossil whales in the desert.
  • Kharga & Dakhla (Western Desert) — a Persian-era temple, a Coptic necropolis and an Islamic mud-brick village.
  • Al-Qasr & Siwa — living heritage, not ruins.

Suggested experience: 3–5 days, slow travel, local guides, early mornings, and time to talk to people.

BEFORE AFTER

Why restoration is necessary

Salt, groundwater, humidity from visitors, sand, heat and time itself attack stone, plaster and pigment. Doing nothing is not a neutral option — it is a decision to lose the object slowly.

What conservation means

Documentation, analysis, stabilisation and, when needed, treatment. Every material added must be reversible, so a future conservator can undo today's work. Restoration is not decoration — it is care with a paper trail.

Why prevention comes first

Controlling humidity, light, vibration and visitor numbers is cheaper and safer than repairing damage later. Preventive conservation protects the original fabric — the part that can never be replaced.

Tourism as a funding loop

Ticket income, licensed guiding, museums and responsible spending pay for conservation teams and equipment. Visitors are not the enemy of heritage — unmanaged, unaware visiting is.

Responsible visitor actions

Seven habits of a Guardian

  • Do not touch fragile surfaces. Skin oils and pressure damage pigment and plaster permanently.
  • Follow site instructions. Barriers, timed entry and photography rules exist for structural reasons.
  • Never remove stones, sherds or objects. A "souvenir" is a permanent loss of evidence.
  • Respect restricted areas. Closed tombs are usually closed because humidity or conservation work demands it.
  • Keep heritage locations clean. Take waste with you; plastic and graffiti cost real money to remove.
  • Share information responsibly. Do not publish exact locations of unguarded sites or encourage access to them.
  • Support responsible tourism. Hire licensed guides, buy from local makers, and choose operators that protect sites.
Did you know?

Restoration is measured in millimetres and months.

A single square metre of painted wall can take conservators months: cleaning, consolidating, filling, retouching only where necessary — and then documenting all of it.

Responsible tourism

Be a Guardian

Heritage is not protected by fences alone. It is protected by the behaviour of the millions of people who travel to see it — including you.

Respect

Lower your voice in tombs, dress appropriately at religious sites, and ask before photographing people.

Protect

Never touch painted surfaces, never climb closed structures, and never take anything — not even a chip of stone.

Learn

Understand what you are looking at before you arrive. Context turns a photo into a memory and a memory into advocacy.

Share responsibly

Celebrate Egypt without exposing fragile or unguarded sites, and correct misinformation when you see it.
